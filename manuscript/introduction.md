# Introduction (draft; citations are literature-matrix ids, to be checked against full text before submission)

STK11 and KEAP1 alterations are common in non-squamous NSCLC and are associated with poor outcomes on PD-(L)1 inhibitors, particularly in KRAS-mutant tumours [L01, L02, L04]. Whether this reflects a prognostic effect or a true reduction in benefit from immunotherapy remains disputed: in a large real-world cohort the associations were similar with and without checkpoint inhibition and formal interaction tests were null [L03], and exploratory analyses of randomised trials did not show loss of pembrolizumab benefit in mutant subgroups [L09, L10].

A separate, clinically pressing question is whether adding platinum chemotherapy to a PD-(L)1 inhibitor matters more for these patients. Pooled regulatory and real-world comparisons of monotherapy and chemo-immunotherapy have focused on PD-L1 and have not been able to examine STK11/KEAP1 [L26, L28, L30]; one two-centre propensity-weighted study reported that no genomic factor conferred differential benefit [L25]. Randomised evidence by genotype is not available [L31, L32].

Most such analyses compare hazard ratios across subgroups, do not anchor biomarker ascertainment to treatment start, and do not address delayed entry of sequenced patients, which biases survival estimates in clinico-genomic cohorts [L41]. We therefore specified a target trial [L45] and aimed to estimate, on the absolute restricted-mean-survival scale [L46], the interaction between pretreatment STK11/KEAP1 status and first-line treatment strategy.
